Parameter Extraction & the Governing PDE

The Black-Scholes-Merton PDE

∂V/∂t  +  ½σ²S² ∂²V/∂S²  +  (r − q)S ∂V/∂S  −  rV  =  0
  • ∂V/∂t — Theta, time decay.
  • ½σ²S² ∂²V/∂S² — the Gamma / convexity term: the "cost" of hedging.
  • (r − q)S ∂V/∂S — the drift / cost-of-carry adjustment.
  • −rV — discounting of the option value.

Closed-Form Solution

C = S e−qT N(d₁) − K e−rT N(d₂)
P = K e−rT N(−d₂) − S e−qT N(−d₁)
d₁ = [ ln(S/K) + (r − q + σ²/2)T ] / (σ√T)
d₂ = d₁ − σ√T

N(d₁) is the Hedge Ratio (Delta) — the exact quantity of the underlying required for risk-neutral replication. N(d₂) is the risk-neutral probability that the option expires in-the-money under the ℚ-measure.

First Principles: GBM, Itô's Lemma & Replication

dS = μS dt + σS dW

The underlying follows Geometric Brownian Motion, where dW is a Wiener process representing market noise. Itô's Lemma is the bridge between this stochastic process and the deterministic option price — it maps the randomness of the stock onto the option's value.

The Replication Argument: by maintaining a Delta-neutral portfolio (short the option, long Δ shares), a trader eliminates the dW term. This self-financing, riskless portfolio must earn the risk-free rate r to preclude arbitrage — justifying Risk-Neutral Valuation: price the option as if the world were risk-neutral, regardless of actual investor risk appetite.

Numerical Approximation for N(x) — Abramowitz & Stegun

Since N(x) has no closed-form integral, this site (and most production pricing engines) uses the high-precision polynomial approximation:

N(x) ≈ 1 − φ(x)(a₁k + a₂k² + a₃k³ + a₄k⁴ + a₅k⁵),   x ≥ 0
k = 1 / (1 + 0.2316419x),   φ(x) = (1/√2π) e−x²/2

a₁=0.31938  a₂=−0.35656  a₃=1.78147  a₄=−1.82125  a₅=1.33027

Algorithmic Workflow

  1. Ingest real-time feeds for S, K, T, σ, r, q.
  2. Calculate intermediate nodes d₁ and d₂.
  3. Approximate N(x) via the Abramowitz & Stegun polynomial.
  4. Solve the closed-form Call/Put equations.
  5. Compute the Greek matrix for real-time risk monitoring.

Data Integrity Mandate: stale volatility or rate data creates model risk. In high-frequency environments, a 1-basis-point error in r or a 1% lag in σ can invalidate an entire Delta hedge.
